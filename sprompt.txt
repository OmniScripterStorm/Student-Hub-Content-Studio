## 1. Core Definition & Governing Principles
A **piecewise-defined function** is a function specified by distinct sub-functions across non-overlapping sub-intervals of its domain.

## 2. Mathematical Formulation
**Piecewise Function General Form**
$$ f(x) = \begin{cases} g(x), & x \in D_1 \\ h(x), & x \in D_2 \end{cases} $$
> *Note: $D_1 \cap D_2 = \emptyset$ ensures functional well-definedness (single output per input).*

## 3. Comparison & Boundary Condition Matrix
| Condition | Notation | Endpoint Marker | Function Value Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strict Inequality | $x < c$ or $x > c$ | Open Circle ($\circ$) | Excluded from branch |
| Inclusive Inequality | $x \le c$ or $x \ge c$ | Solid Circle ($\bullet$) | Included in branch ($f(c)$ defined) |

## 5. Worked Exemplar Problem
### Problem Statement
Evaluate $f(1)$ and determine if $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = 1$ for:
$$ f(x) = \begin{cases} -x + 2, & x \le 1 \\ x^2, & x > 1 \end{cases} $$

### Step-by-Step Derivation
1. **Identify Relevant Branch for $x = 1$**: Since $1 \le 1$, substitute into branch 1: $f(1) = -(1) + 2 = 1$.
2. **Evaluate Left-Hand Limit**: $\lim_{x \to 1^-} (-x + 2) = 1$.
3. **Evaluate Right-Hand Limit**: $\lim_{x \to 1^+} (x^2) = 1^2 = 1$.
4. **Continuity Verification**: Since $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = f(1) = 1$, the function is continuous at $x = 1$.

## 6. Key Takeaways & Exam Strategy
- **Domain Matching**: Always check domain constraints first before substituting $x$.
- **Boundary Discontinuities**: If left-hand and right-hand limits differ at boundary $x = c$, a jump discontinuity exists.
- **Cartesian Plotting**: Always draw solid circles for $\le / \ge$ and hollow circles for $< / >$.
